I find mentoring early-career scientists, especially in small groups embedded in
large collaborations, to be the most rewarding part of my work. A community of
mentors and peers was essential to my own path, and I try to build that for the
students I work with: clear projects with real stakes, regular one-on-one time,
and connections into the broader Rubin, DESC, and DELVE communities so that no
student depends on a single advisor.

## Students

Since 2018 I have been the primary research mentor for nine undergraduate and
graduate students. Two of these projects led to first-author publications by the
student.

- **Emma Lamb-Smith** (2026–present), astronomy major, University of Washington.
  Machine-learning identification of cluster-scale strong lenses in LSST data.
- **Matthieu Plessier** (2025–present), physics PhD student, Université Grenoble Alpes.
  Impact of the observational selection function on stellar-stream density measurements.
- **Elliott Burdett** (2024–present), physics major, University of Washington.
  Searching for new stellar streams in DELVE and building scalable stream-search code for LSST.
- **Shriya Prasanna** (2024–present), physics major, University of Washington.
  Modeling stellar streams and fitting the orbits of their progenitors.
- **Kyle Boone** (2022–2025), physics major, University of Wisconsin–Madison.
  Synthetic source injection and survey property maps to correct stream densities for
  selection effects; first-author paper
  [Boone, Ferguson et al. 2026](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026ApJ..1001..208B).
  Now a graduate student at Harvard.
- **Miranda Gorsuch** (2023–2024), physics PhD student, University of Wisconsin–Madison.
  Data analysis for LSST commissioning.
- **Kiyan Tavangar** (2020–2021), astrophysics major, University of Chicago.
  Characterizing stellar streams in DES; first-author paper
  [Tavangar, Ferguson et al. 2022](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...925..118T).
  Now a graduate student at Columbia.
- **Leo Barba** (2018–2021), physics major, Texas A&M University.
  Designed and 3D-printed components for TCal and helped commission and run the instrument.
  Now a graduate student at UMass Amherst.
- **Sarah Hughes** (2018), REU student, Texas A&M University.
  Designed the LabVIEW control software for TCal.

At the University of Washington I co-run a research group on stellar streams and dark
matter with [Nora Shipp](https://nora-shipp.github.io/).
