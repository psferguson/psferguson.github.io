My research focuses on the nature of dark matter and the assembly history of our
galaxy, the Milky Way. Dwarf galaxies and stellar streams (the tidal remnants of
disrupted satellites) let us count and weigh dark matter halos down to, and below,
the mass scale where galaxies stop forming, which is where competing dark matter
models disagree most. Finding these faint structures and measuring them well enough
to compare with theory requires wide-field surveys with carefully characterized
selection functions, so my science work is tightly coupled to my
[instrumentation and data systems]({{site.baseurl}}/instrumentation/) work.

I am an in-kind contributor to the Vera C. Rubin Observatory, a builder of the DECam
Local Volume Exploration Survey ([**DELVE**](https://delve-survey.github.io)), and a
member of the Dark Energy Survey ([**DES**](https://www.darkenergysurvey.org/)), the
Dark Energy Science Collaboration ([**DESC**](https://lsstdesc.org/), where I served
as co-convener of the Dark Matter Working Group), and the Southern Stellar Stream
Spectroscopic Survey ([**S<sup>5</sup>**](https://s5collab.github.io/)).

## Stellar streams as probes of dark matter

Density variations and gaps along thin stellar streams record gravitational encounters
with dark subhalos, making streams the only known way to measure dark matter clustering
in the Milky Way below the threshold of galaxy formation. I discover and characterize
streams in deep photometric surveys, model them in realistic Milky Way potentials, and
build the tools needed to separate real perturbations from observational systematics.

Selected papers:

- [No Stream Left Unscathed: The Imprint of a Host Galaxy](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026ApJ..1008...91A) (Arora, **Ferguson**, et al. 2026): how the Milky Way's own structure perturbs streams, which must be modeled before subhalo signals can be claimed.
- [Robust Measurement of Stellar Streams around the Milky Way](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026ApJ..1001..208B) (<u>Boone</u>, **Ferguson**, et al. 2026): correcting stream densities for spatially variable survey selection effects using synthetic source injection.
- [Sifting for a Stream: The Morphology of the 300S Stellar Stream](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026OJAp....961089C) (Cohen, Ji, **Ferguson**, et al. 2026).
- [The DECam Field of Streams](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250605469F) (**Ferguson** & Shipp 2025): a deep, contiguous view of the southern Milky Way halo from DES and DELVE.
- [DELVE-ing into the Jet: A Thin Stellar Stream on a Retrograde Orbit at 30 kpc](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022AJ....163...18F) (**Ferguson** et al. 2022) and [From the Fire: A Deeper Look at the Phoenix Stream](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...925..118T) (<u>Tavangar</u>, **Ferguson**, et al. 2022).
- [S<sup>5</sup>: The Orbital and Chemical Properties of One Dozen Stellar Streams](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...928...30L) (Li, Ji, Pace, ..., **Ferguson**, et al. 2022) and [Stellar Streams Discovered in the Dark Energy Survey](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...862..114S) (Shipp et al. 2018).

## Milky Way satellites and the local volume

The census of ultra-faint dwarf galaxies around the Milky Way and the Magellanic Clouds
constrains the dark matter halo mass function at the smallest scales that host stars.
Through DELVE I have helped discover and confirm many new satellites, and I work on the
survey selection functions that turn a list of discoveries into a population that can be
compared to simulations. I am now extending this to the first Rubin data and to
semi-resolved field dwarfs at 2–5 Mpc, using machine-learning classifiers trained on
synthetically injected sources.

Selected papers:

- [DELVE Milky Way Satellite Galaxy Census I: Satellite Population and Survey Selection Function](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026ApJ..1000...87T) (Tan et al. 2026) and [Ultra-faint Milky Way Satellites Discovered in Carina, Phoenix, and Telescopium with DELVE DR3](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026ApJ..1000...46T) (Tan et al. 2026).
- [Predictions for the Detectability of Milky Way Satellite Galaxies and Outer-Halo Star Clusters with Rubin](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025OJAp....8E..89T) (Tsiane, Mau, Drlica-Wagner, ..., **Ferguson**, et al. 2025).
- [Discovery of the Distant, Ultra-faint Milky Way Satellite Aquarius IV with Rubin Early Data Preview 2](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026RNAAS..10..239C) (Cerny et al. 2026): one of the first new satellites found in Rubin data.
- [Six More Ultra-faint Milky Way Companions Discovered in DELVE](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...953....1C) (Cerny et al. 2023), and the discovery papers for [Eridanus IV](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...920L..44C), [Pegasus IV](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...942..111C), [Aquarius III](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...979..164C), and [Leo VI](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...979..176T).
- [DELVE-ing into the Milky Way's Globular Clusters](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025AJ....170..294C) (Chiti, Tavangar, **Ferguson**, et al. 2025): extratidal features around NGC 5897 and NGC 7492.
- [A New Faint, Diffuse Dwarf Satellite of NGC 55](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...961..126M) (McNanna et al. 2024) and [DELVE-DEEP: The Faint Satellite System of NGC 55](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...990..108M) (Medoff et al. 2025): satellites beyond the Milky Way.
- [Three-dimensional Structure of the Sagittarius Dwarf Spheroidal Core from RR Lyrae](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.495.4124F) (**Ferguson** & Strigari 2020).
