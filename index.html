Welcome!

I'm an astronomer who builds the calibrations, validation tools, and data systems behind wide-field sky surveys, and uses those surveys to study the nature of dark matter through the stellar streams and dwarf galaxies of the Milky Way and its neighbors. I have worked on every stage of the pipeline, from building instruments and commissioning telescopes to delivering science-ready catalogs.

Currently, I am a DiRAC Fellow in the University of Washington Astronomy Department and an in-kind contributor to the Vera C. Rubin Observatory. During commissioning I was a core member of the system-integration analysis team, spent 2023 on the summit in Chile, and led the sample-production science unit. Now that the Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) is underway, I am using its deluge of data to find and characterize substructure in the Milky Way halo. Previously, I was a research associate in the observational cosmology group at the University of Wisconsin–Madison working with Keith Bechtol. I received my PhD from Texas A&M University in 2021.

Most of my work is done in science collaborations that include:

  • Vera C. Rubin Observatory (LSST)
  • Dark Energy Science Collaboration (DESC)
  • DECam Local Volume Exploration Survey (DELVE)
  • Dark Energy Survey (DES)
  • Southern Stellar Stream Spectroscopic Survey (S5)

I am also deeply interested in helping others succeed and transforming academia into a more inclusive and supportive environment for all.

In my free time I play clawhammer banjo, ultimate frisbee, listen to entirely too much music and figure out how to spend more time in nature.

For more details, keep exploring my website or check out my CV.
